Editorial

  • Project:
    • The DHARMA project received funding from the European Research Council (ERC) under the European Union’s Horizon 2020 research and innovation programme (grant agreement no 809994).

    • This private provisional edition is prepared as part of ongoing doctoral research at the École Pratique des Hautes Études (EPHE–PSL); its textual constitution and critical apparatus have not yet been finalized.

  • Editorial declaration:
    • correction:
      • The text of the provisional base is retained as supplied; no Sanskrit or Old Javanese emendations were introduced in this structural revision. The segmentation and philological status of some passages will need to be checked against the witnesses.
    • normalization:
      • The provisional base text transcribes the section “Base text for conversion to XML” in the editor’s working DOCX, retaining its preliminary normalized readings and editorial annotations without imposing further textual normalization. The Sanskrit stanza readings are not yet critically established; manuscript variants remain to be collated.
    • interpretation:
      • The source document combines Old Javanese prose with Sanskrit stanzas. Source folio tags [L:...] and [JB:...] were converted to page-break milestones. The DOCX contains preliminary readings and collation observations; these are preserved as XML working comments, not encoded as reviewed textual variants. Source numbering (including duplicate or absent dyad numbers) has been retained. The metrical attribution of stanza segments remains provisional, and their line divisions still need checking.

Witnesses

  • [L] Leiden, the Netherlands, Leiden University Libraries, Or. 5037
    • Physical Description: Palm-leaf manuscript from the Lombok Collection, measuring 48 cm and comprising 62 folios according to the catalogue. It contains a complete copy of the juridical Sārasamuccaya, with a copying colophon dated Śaka 1658 (1736 CE). A separate Roman-script transcription associated with Soegiarto is available but is treated as a derivative representation of the same witness, not as an independent witness.
    • Hand Description: A detailed description of the script and scribal hand has not yet been prepared.
  • [JB] Tabanan, Bali, Indonesia, Griya Jero Bakungan,
    • Physical Description: Palm-leaf manuscript associated with Jero Bakungan, its text can also be accessed through HKS 5269, a twentieth-century Roman-script transcription of the manuscript. The transcription preserves the characteristic opening of the juridical Sārasamuccaya. The Sārasamuccaya səmpiran closing formula occurs within a larger compilation, which continues with further juridical material. HKS 5269 is a documentary surrogate for the Jero Bakungan manuscript, not an independent witness.
    • Hand Description: The hand of the palm-leaf exemplar has not been examined; orthographic details recorded in HKS 5269 require verification against the exemplar.
  • [K] Singaraja, Bali, Indonesia, Kirtya, II.a 940/6
    • Physical Description: Palm-leaf manuscript consisting of 21 folios, transmitting the juridical Sārasamuccaya. The manuscript has covers bearing handwritten inscriptions, a Kirtya collection stamp, and a catalogue label identifying it as II A/6/940. The cover also bears the title Sārasamuccaya–Səmpiran and the date 20 January 1933, whose significance remains to be determined. A twentieth-century Roman-script transcription is preserved as Leiden Or. 9537, previously the principal source for this witness. The relationship between the transcription and its palm-leaf exemplar will be checked through direct comparison.
    • Hand Description: Description of the script and scribal hand pending examination of the palm-leaf manuscript.
  • [B] Leiden, the Netherlands, Leiden University Libraries, Or. 6203a
    • Physical Description: Roman-script transcription associated with the juridical Sārasamuccaya material designated Br. 144a and Batavia cod. no. 144, a larger collection of Old Javanese legal texts. A note associated with this material describes a Latin-script copy supplied with word division, division into articles, and several corrections by Brandes. A working transcription of Leiden Or. 6203a has been completed, but its precise documentary relationship to Br. 144a and Batavia cod. no. 144 remains to be verified. The siglum B designates this textual witness as presently represented by the available copy, not an additional independent manuscript.
    • Hand Description: The scribal characteristics of the underlying material have not been established. Word division and other features of the available copy may reflect later transcriptional or editorial intervention.
  • [KJ] Singaraja, Bali, Indonesia, Kirtya, IIIb 6924
    • Physical Description: Witness associated with Karang Jasi, Lombok, presently accessible through a Roman-script copy. Its contents confirm that it transmits the juridical Sārasamuccaya. The available copy includes an ending formula followed by a copying notice dated Śaka 1858, referring to a lontar associated with I Vayan Cenara of Karang Jasi; further babad material follows in the documentary copy. The sequence of larger textual sections differs in some respects from Leiden Or. 5037. Its textual affiliation remains to be established through collation.
    • Hand Description: The script and hand of the underlying lontar have not been directly examined; the present textual evidence is mediated through a Roman-script copy.
  • [R] Leiden, the Netherlands, Leiden University Libraries, Or. 3796 (Br. 996)
    • Physical Description: Palm-leaf multitext manuscript, measuring 37 cm and comprising 16 folios according to the catalogue. Its first textual unit contains Lvir niṅ apitan; the juridical Sārasamuccaya, also designated Sarasamudaya, follows. The beginning of the Sārasamuccaya portion is missing, so this witness cannot be used for the shared opening preserved elsewhere. The manuscript contains dating statements associated in the literature with Śaka 1761, 1762, and 1764, but their interpretation requires verification. A direct working transliteration is in progress, and the extent of corresponding legal material remains to be mapped.
    • Hand Description: A detailed description of the script and scribal hand has not yet been prepared.
